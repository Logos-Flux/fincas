## 0. About this release

v0.4.4 adds customer deposits to the general ledger and makes a payment
recorded through the API settle its order. It is v0.4.3 plus that one
change, for deployments that take payment before they invoice (a storefront
card sale invoiced at shipment):

- **Customer deposits (opt-in).** Name a liability as the payment rule's
  deposit account, and a payment with no invoice credits it instead of A/R.
  When the invoice the payment is linked to is issued, the amount moves to
  A/R. Until you set the account, postings are exactly as in v0.4.3.
- **API payments settle the order.** A payment created or updated through
  `/api/v1/payments/payments/` now recomputes its order's `amount_paid`,
  `balance_due` and `payment_status`. Before, an API prepayment left the
  order unpaid.

One migration (reversible), no new settings and no new required secrets. The
dependency ranges are those of v0.4.3 (Django 6.0). Install with
`pip install fincas==0.4.4`; the import name stays `golden_erp`.

## 1. Before you upgrade

Upgrading from v0.4.3:

1. Install the new version and run `manage.py migrate`. It applies
   `accounting.0004_deposit_account` (one nullable column on the posting
   rules, and one more journal entry source). Expect
   `Applying accounting.0004_deposit_account... OK`.
2. **Nothing changes in the ledger until you opt in.** To hold prepayments
   as customer deposits, name a liability account on the payment rule, and
   if card funds sit with a processor before the payout, name its clearing
   account:

   ```bash
   python manage.py posting_rules set --doc-type payment --deposit "Customer Deposits"
   python manage.py posting_rules set-provider --provider stripe --cash "Stripe clearing"
   ```

   The deposit account must be a liability in the rule's company.
   `--no-deposit` clears it.
3. **If you record payments through the API against orders**, those orders
   now show as paid once the payment settles. Orders paid that way before
   the upgrade keep their old `amount_paid` until a payment on them is next
   saved.
4. Run `manage.py check_money` after the first deposits are applied; it
   reports `deposit_overapplied` when a deposit account has a debit balance.

From v0.4.1 or v0.4.2, follow the v0.4.2 notes first
([docs/releases/v0.4.2.md](v0.4.2.md) section 1).

## 2. Behavior changes staff will notice

- An order paid through the API (a storefront checkout that records its card
  payment) shows **Paid** with the amount, where it used to show unpaid.
- With a deposit account set:
  - a payment taken before the invoice appears in the ledger under customer
    deposits, not as a credit on A/R;
  - converting the order to its invoice links the order's held deposits to
    the draft invoice;
  - issuing the invoice posts a **Deposit Application** journal entry per
    linked deposit (DR deposits, CR A/R);
  - voiding the invoice or the payment, or moving the payment to another
    invoice, reverses that entry;
  - a refund of a deposit that was never applied debits deposits, and one
    that was applied debits A/R.
- A payment that already has an invoice when it settles still credits A/R
  directly.

## 3. Known limitations

- A deposit applies whole: its gross amount, less refunds already taken from
  deposits, goes to the one invoice it is linked to. It cannot be split
  across invoices. An overpayment leaves a credit on A/R.
- The payout from the processor to the bank is not posted; it is bank
  reconciliation, as before.
- The limitations in the v0.4.2 notes still apply
  ([docs/releases/v0.4.2.md](v0.4.2.md) section 3).
